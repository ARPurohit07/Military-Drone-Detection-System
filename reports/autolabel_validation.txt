Auto-labeler validation: Grounding DINO boxes vs. human annotations
===================================================================

Method
------
The zero-shot auto-labeler is scored against the 1325 hand-annotated boxes in
the legacy dataset (drone_dataset_3_test). For each image the highest-scoring
detection is compared to the best-matching human box by IoU. This measures only
box geometry ("where"); the class label comes from the airframe registry via the
scrape manifest, never from the detector.

"clean-GT" excludes images whose human box covers more than 75% of the frame.
There are 118 such boxes in the legacy dataset and many are lazy annotations, so
disagreeing with them is not necessarily an error on the detector's part.

Model: IDEA-Research/grounding-dino-tiny (172M params)
Threshold: 0.25 box / 0.25 text
Sample: 80 images (74 excluding near-full-frame GT), seed 0

An earlier 150-image run of the three-phrase prompt agreed with this ordering
(mean 0.582, median 0.656, 70.0% at IoU>=0.5, 0 misses).

Conclusion
----------
The two-phrase prompt "a drone. an unmanned aerial vehicle." is what the
pipeline uses. Adding "an aircraft." measurably hurts - it pulls the box toward
whole-airframe and plane-like regions, costing 12 points of IoU>=0.5 - so the
prompt is kept deliberately narrow.

Miss rate is 0% across all three prompts: the detector finds something in every
image. That means auto-labelling never silently drops a drone image, which is the
failure that would matter most (an unlabelled drone image trains as background).

Recall against a human annotator is ~80% at IoU>=0.5, not ~100%. The pipeline is
built around that gap rather than ignoring it:

  - Detections scoring below 0.40 are written with VOC's `difficult` flag and
    skipped during training (config.TrainConfig.skip_difficult), so training sees
    only the boxes the detector was confident about.
  - Boxes covering >75% of the frame, degenerate boxes and sub-8px boxes are
    rejected outright, which is the specific defect class the legacy dataset
    carried 118 of.
  - Every annotation records the airframe, query and annotator model, so the
    labelling can be re-derived or audited later from the XML alone.


Resolution and throughput
=========================

Grounding DINO's image processor defaults to shortest_edge=800 /
longest_edge=1333 and will UPSCALE to reach it. Pre-resizing the PIL image was
therefore silently undone, and peak memory sat at 3.71 GB of this card's
4.29 GB. Resolution has to be set on the processor to have any effect.

Measured on 40 legacy images with clean human boxes, GPU otherwise idle:

config              batch   ms/img   peak VRAM   mean IoU   IoU>=0.5   7k imgs
default 800/1333      2       1081     3.71 GB     0.608      80.0%    126 min
480/800               2        309     1.85 GB     0.608      77.5%     36 min
480/800               4        335     2.89 GB     0.608      77.5%     39 min

Shipped: 480/800 at batch 2 (autolabel.PROCESSOR_SIZE).

3.5x faster and half the memory for 2.5 points of IoU>=0.5, with mean IoU
unchanged to three decimals. Batch 4 is marginally *slower* than batch 2 and
uses 56% more memory - past a point the batch is padded to its largest member
and the extra parallelism does not pay for the extra pixels.

480/800 is not only the fast option, it is the principled one: it matches
TrainConfig.min_size / max_size, so the auto-labeller sees images at the same
resolution the Faster R-CNN being trained will see. Labelling at a resolution
the downstream model never gets has no benefit.

An earlier measurement of 5738 ms/img at this same setting was an artifact of
two benchmark processes sharing the GPU. Any throughput number taken while
another CUDA job is running on a 4 GB card is meaningless - the card was at
3955 MiB of 4096 and thrashing.

CAVEAT - small objects are NOT covered by this measurement. Every one of the 40
sample images has a ground-truth box larger than 5% of the frame; the legacy
dataset contains no small-object annotations at all (its boxes being
indiscriminately large is part of why it needed replacing). Low resolution costs
small distant aircraft first, and that cost is invisible here. If per-class AP
later comes out weak for the small hand-launched types (RQ-11 Raven, Orlan-10,
Skylark, Aladin), raising PROCESSOR_SIZE for those airframes is the first thing
to try.
